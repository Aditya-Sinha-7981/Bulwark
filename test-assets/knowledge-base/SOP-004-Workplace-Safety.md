# SOP-004 — Workplace Safety: PPE, Permit-to-Work, Lockout/Tagout, and Emergency Procedures

**Document type:** Standard Operating Procedure
**Applicable scope:** All personnel and contractors performing work within TTPS plant boundaries
**Issuing authority:** Trishul Thermal Power Station (TTPS) — Health, Safety & Environment Department
**Revision:** 5
**Effective date:** 2026-03-01

> This is a synthetic, fictional procedure created for internal system testing. It does not describe any real facility, organization, or equipment.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines mandatory personal protective equipment (PPE), permit-to-work (PTW) requirements, lockout/tagout (LOTO) steps, isolation verification methods, emergency response actions, and restricted work conditions applicable across the plant. Equipment-specific isolation detail (valves, pressure vessels) is defined in SOP-002 and SOP-003 respectively and cross-references this SOP for the LOTO mechanics themselves.

## 2. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

- **Baseline PPE, mandatory in all plant areas without exception:** safety helmet, safety shoes (steel toe-cap), safety glasses.
- **Task-specific additional PPE:**
  - Hearing protection: mandatory in any area posted as exceeding 85 dB(A), including the pump house during pump operation.
  - Face shield: mandatory during mechanical seal work, grinding, or any task with a splash/particle-ejection risk.
  - Self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA): mandatory for confined space entry where the atmosphere is expected or found to be oxygen-deficient.
  - Insulated gloves and arc-rated clothing: mandatory for any work on live low-voltage electrical equipment (work on live equipment above low voltage is prohibited outright, not merely PPE-gated).

## 3. Permit-to-Work (PTW) System

| Permit type | Required for |
|---|---|
| General PTW | Routine mechanical/electrical maintenance not covered by a specialized permit below |
| Hot Work Permit | Welding, grinding, cutting, or any spark/flame-producing activity |
| Confined Space Entry Permit | Entry into any vessel, pit, or enclosed space per Section 6 |
| Height Work Permit | Any work performed at 1.8 m or more above a walking/working surface |

- A permit is valid for a single shift, up to a maximum of **12 hours**, unless explicitly extended and re-endorsed in writing by the Shift Engineer before expiry.
- The permit must be physically displayed at the worksite for the duration of the work.
- Work shall stop immediately if the permit expires and has not been re-endorsed, regardless of how close the task is to completion.

## 4. Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) — Standard Steps

1. Notify all personnel affected by the equipment shutdown.
2. Shut down the equipment using its normal stopping procedure.
3. Identify and isolate **all** energy sources — electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, and pneumatic — not only the primary energy source for the equipment's normal function.
4. Apply a personal lock and a signed, dated tag at each isolation point identified in step 3.
5. Verify a zero-energy state at each isolation point (see Section 5).
6. Perform the work.
7. Remove locks in the reverse order they were applied, and only by the person who applied each lock.

### Lock Removal Authorization (exception path)

If the person who applied a lock is unavailable to remove it (e.g., off shift, off site), the lock may only be removed via the Lock Removal Authorization procedure: the Area Engineer independently re-verifies the zero-energy state and the completion of the associated work, documents this verification, and only then authorizes removal of the lock in the applying person's absence. A lock shall never be removed by anyone else without this documented independent re-verification.

## 5. Isolation Verification

- **Electrical isolation:** verify absence of voltage using a calibrated voltage detector, proven against a known live source both immediately before and immediately after testing the isolated point (a "prove dead, test, prove dead" sequence).
- **Mechanical/fluid isolation (valves):** verified per SOP-002's isolation adequacy test — double block and bleed, or a blank/spade, confirmed on an Isolation Certificate.
- **Pressure vessel entry:** the vessel must be drained and depressurized to atmospheric pressure per SOP-003 before entry, in addition to the confined-space requirements in Section 6 of this SOP.

## 6. Confined Space Entry

- Continuous atmosphere monitoring is required throughout the entry: oxygen 19.5%–23.5% by volume, and Lower Explosive Limit (LEL) below 10%.
- A dedicated attendant remains stationed outside the confined space at all times during the entry, in continuous communication with those inside.
- Entry shall not begin, and shall be stopped if already in progress, if either atmospheric parameter above falls outside its stated range.

## 7. Emergency Procedures

- **Fire alarm:** stop work immediately; isolate energy sources if it is safe to do so; proceed to the designated Assembly Point (Assembly Point 3 — Main Gate, unless a more specific point is posted for the work area).
- **Gas or toxic release alarm:** evacuate upwind of the release toward the designated Assembly Point per the site emergency plan.
- Do not re-enter the affected area until an "All Clear" has been given by the Emergency Response Team — a decreasing alarm tone or apparent quiet is not sufficient authorization to re-enter.

## 8. Restricted Work Conditions

- **Hot work near fuel storage:** no hot work is permitted within 15 metres of a fuel storage area without a Hot Work Permit specifically cleared by gas testing, and such a permit is valid for a maximum of **2 hours** at a time, with a re-test of the atmosphere every **30 minutes** for the duration of the work.
- **Confined space entry:** never permitted without continuous atmosphere monitoring (Section 6) and a dedicated attendant; a permit alone, without the attendant physically present, does not authorize entry.
