# SOP-003 — Pressure Vessel Inspection

**Document type:** Standard Operating Procedure
**Applicable equipment:** Stationary pressure vessels rated up to 10.5 kg/cm² design pressure (e.g. compressed-air receivers, drain flash vessels) — excludes boiler pressure parts, which are covered under a separate statutory inspection scheme not addressed by this SOP
**Issuing authority:** Trishul Thermal Power Station (TTPS) — Mechanical Maintenance Department
**Revision:** 2
**Effective date:** 2025-11-20

> This is a synthetic, fictional procedure created for internal system testing. It does not describe any real facility, organization, or equipment.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines inspection intervals, visual and corrosion inspection criteria, pressure-related findings, escalation criteria, and documentation requirements for the pressure vessels listed in the plant's Pressure Equipment Register.

## 2. Inspection Intervals

| Inspection type | Interval |
|---|---|
| External visual inspection (in service) | Every 6 months |
| Internal inspection (vessel opened, de-pressurized, and entered per SOP-004) | Every 24 months, or per statutory requirement, whichever is sooner |
| Ultrasonic thickness (UT) survey | Every 48 months |
| Pressure relief valve set-pressure verification | At every internal inspection, and independently every 12 months |

Internal inspection requires the vessel to be isolated, drained, and depressurized to atmospheric pressure, and entry is conducted per SOP-004's confined-space and isolation-verification requirements.

## 3. Visual Inspection

External and internal visual inspection shall record: general corrosion or pitting, coating/lining condition, evidence of bulging or distortion, condition of all nozzle welds and the manway/inspection cover gasket seating surface, and support/foundation condition.

## 4. Corrosion Findings — Wall Thickness Classification

Remaining wall thickness is measured by UT survey at the designated measurement points and compared against the vessel's design minimum thickness (**t-min**, from the vessel's original design/fabrication record).

| Remaining thickness | Classification | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 1.25 × t-min | Satisfactory | No action; continue normal interval |
| ≥ 1.0 × t-min and < 1.25 × t-min | Monitor | Re-inspect (UT survey) within **12 months** instead of the standard 48-month interval |
| < 1.0 × t-min | Critical | Vessel shall be removed from service or de-rated to a reduced working pressure, pending a formal engineering assessment. It shall not be returned to its original design pressure without that assessment. |

## 5. Pressure-Related Findings

- **Relief valve set pressure:** verified against the vessel's nameplate set pressure with a tolerance of **±3%**. A relief valve found outside this tolerance shall be re-set or replaced before the vessel is returned to service.
- **Evidence of exceeding MAWP:** any evidence that the vessel has operated above its Maximum Allowable Working Pressure (MAWP) — including a popped/lifted relief valve with no other explanation, or a pressure gauge log entry above MAWP — requires immediate shutdown and isolation of the vessel pending inspection.
- **Bulging or blistering:** any visually observed bulging or blistering of the shell or heads is treated as a Critical finding under Section 4, regardless of the last UT survey result, and requires immediate shutdown.
- A vessel exhibiting either exceeded-MAWP evidence or bulging/blistering shall not be restarted until a competent person (a person formally authorized under the plant's pressure-equipment competency scheme) has examined it and cleared it for service in writing.

## 6. Escalation Criteria

- Any **Critical** finding under Section 4 or Section 5 (sub-t-min thickness, exceeded MAWP, or bulging/blistering): report to the Site Safety Officer and the Statutory Compliance Cell within **2 hours** of the finding.
- A relief valve found outside the ±3% set-pressure tolerance, with no other Critical finding present, is escalated to the Area Engineer within 24 hours (not the 2-hour Critical path) and scheduled for re-set/replacement before the vessel returns to service.
- A vessel in the "Monitor" thickness classification is not escalated beyond scheduling the earlier 12-month re-inspection; it remains in service in the interim.

## 7. Documentation Requirements

- Every inspection (visual, internal, or UT survey) is recorded on the standard Pressure Vessel Inspection Report and filed in the Pressure Equipment Register.
- UT survey raw thickness data (all measurement points, not just the minimum) is attached to the inspection report, not summarized away.
- Inspection records are retained for the operating life of the vessel plus **5 years** after decommissioning.
- Any Critical finding and its resulting engineering assessment or de-rating decision is filed as a permanent attachment to the vessel's Pressure Equipment Register entry, not just in the routine inspection report.
